Description

Why this context exists, in business language. No technical detail.

Coordinates picking, packing and shipping once an order is paid. Owns the shipment lifecycle from payment confirmation to carrier hand-off.

Domain roles

How does this context behave? Analysis, execution, gateway…

  • Execution context
  • Gateway context
Carries out the fulfillment workflow from paid order to dispatched shipment, and fronts the carrier integrations for the rest of the system.

Ubiquitous language

The key domain terms used inside this context, and what they mean here.

Shipment
A physical parcel dispatched against an order.
Pick List
The ordered set of warehouse locations to visit for one shipment.
Dispatch
The moment a packed shipment is handed to a carrier.
Delivery Promise
The date shown at checkout that fulfillment must honor.

Business decisions

The rules and policies this context owns and enforces.

  • No partial shipments. An order ships complete or not at all.
  • Cost-first carrier selection. The cheapest carrier that meets the delivery promise wins.
  • Hazardous goods go ground. Never booked onto air freight, whatever the promise.

Assumptions

What you are taking on faith. Dashed on purpose — none of this is settled.

  • Warehouse stock counts are accurate within the hour.
  • A single warehouse fulfills any given order.

Verification metrics

Signals that tell you whether this boundary was drawn in the right place.

  • Time from payment to dispatch under 4 hours.
  • Fewer than 1% of shipments re-booked with a second carrier.

Open questions

What nobody in the room could answer. Lots of these means the design is still soft.

  • Who owns returns — this context or a new one?
  • Does the carrier integration deserve its own context?
